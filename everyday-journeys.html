Proposal

This is an independent, community-facing proposal—not an operating service or announced government program. Local people are the source of its purpose and direction; institutions provide necessary implementation, permit and legislative pathways.

Set the rollout order

Use local demand, access, operating and approval evidence to decide which corridors and service windows come first.

Compound every deployment

People choose the destination

Evidence sets the rollout order

The regional goal is clear: upgrade or replace suitable local passenger and delivery services with a coordinated 24/7/365 autonomous network. Local conditions determine which journey starts when and under which implementation profile.

Public-interest design does not invent a particular person’s experience. It turns people’s direction into ranked corridors, service standards, operator roles and measurable operating requirements.

Planning frame

Each card answers four things

  • Local condition: what must be measured in that place.
  • Network role: how the journey strengthens regional coverage.
  • Implementation evidence: what is required for approved operation.
  • Pause or redesign condition: what must change before continuing.

Four priority journey streams

Everyday journey scenarios

These scenarios are specific enough to test, substantial enough to demonstrate real public value and structured for coordinated regional planning. They are priority parts of a system intended to upgrade or replace all suitable local service models over time, not isolated experiments. None identifies a confirmed route, customer group or service gap.

Scenario 1 · Passenger shuttle

Station connection

Local condition to measure
Whether people in a defined catchment experience a first- or last-leg barrier to an existing train or high-frequency bus connection, at times when another suitable option is not available.

Proposal
A booked feeder service linking accessible neighbourhood stops across defined catchments with existing stations or interchanges, planned for dependable day-and-night coverage as the network grows. Timetables would connect with, not compete against, the main service.

Evidence required
Direct resident research; an audit of current routes, walking and wheeling paths, parking and personalised transport; origin-and-destination patterns; safe stop locations; likely transfer times; accessibility requirements; and a measurable demand threshold.

Stop condition
Do not proceed to real-world operation if the demand threshold is not met, a safer existing option can fill the gap, suitable boarding points cannot be secured, or the feeder would reduce rather than improve the whole journey.

Scenario 2 · Passenger shuttle

Health appointment

Local condition to measure
Whether a defined group has recurring, non-emergency appointment journeys that existing public, community, patient and personalised transport options do not serve reliably or accessibly.

Proposal
A pre-booked accessible shuttle to an agreed health destination or public-transport connection. It would be a transport service—not an ambulance, clinical transfer or substitute for care staff.

Evidence required
Patient and carer co-design; health-service support; a lawful, privacy-preserving picture of journey demand; safe handover rules; mobility and assistance needs; appointment timing; existing provider capacity; and boundaries for passengers who need clinical support.

Stop condition
Do not proceed to real-world operation if qualified health and transport partners do not support the model, privacy or handover cannot be protected, the service duplicates an effective option, or passenger needs exceed a non-clinical shuttle’s safe scope.

Scenario 3 · Passenger shuttle

Accessible local trip

Local condition to measure
Whether people with disability, older people, carers or people travelling with mobility aids face a specific barrier on a short local trip that has not been resolved by existing services.

Proposal
A phone- or online-booked local shuttle with an accessible boarding path, vehicle and information service. Assistance animals, mobility devices, carers and extra boarding time would be designed in from the start.

Evidence required
Paid co-design with people who have diverse access needs; an audit against transport accessibility law and guidance; real boarding trials; vehicle and restraint checks; accessible information; phone booking tests; assistance protocols; and dignified service-recovery plans.

Stop condition
Do not proceed to real-world operation if any part of the whole journey is unsafe, inaccessible or undignified; if a person must own a smartphone to participate; or if representative user testing does not support proceeding.

Scenario 4 · Delivery drone

Lightweight health or essential-goods delivery

Local condition to measure
Whether a particular authorised sender and receiving site have a time-sensitive, lightweight delivery task for which an approved drone route would offer a clear benefit over ordinary road delivery.

Proposal
A scheduled service pattern between controlled sites, with enough movements to test operational value, reliability and community impact. Medicines, pathology items or other health-related goods would only be included under qualified service ownership with validated handling and chain-of-custody procedures.

Evidence required
A named service owner; item volumes and urgency; road-delivery baseline; packaging, temperature and handover validation; land permissions; CASA operator and flight approvals; ground and air risk assessment; noise and wildlife review; weather reliability; privacy controls; and community acceptance.

Stop condition
Do not proceed to real-world operation if there is no meaningful service advantage, chain of custody cannot be assured, an approved operator or route cannot be secured, community impacts are unacceptable, or conditions make reliable operation unlikely.

Guide to action

Build the evidence for coordinated action

Passenger and delivery planning can move together towards a regional 24/7/365 operating network without buying technology or naming partners who have not agreed to participate. Each workstream keeps its own operational, safety and approval gates.

  1. Define the priority journey set

    For each journey, define the possible user groups, origin and destination areas, time windows, network role and local evidence gaps.

    Proof to move on: a ranked regional rollout portfolio with evidence gaps and implementation requirements clearly identified.

  2. Map services, capability and local ownership

    Document public transport, walking and wheeling paths, community transport, taxis, health transport and delivery services. Invite existing operators and workers to identify upgrade or replacement opportunities and roles as owners, partners and operators.

    Proof to move on: a regional service and capability map checked by providers, workers and potential users, with no partnership implied before agreement.

  3. Listen with the people affected

    Use accessible, paid and privacy-respecting participation where possible. Do not ask people to endorse autonomous technology; ask them to describe the journey, choices, constraints and acceptable trade-offs.

    Proof to move on: documented themes, disagreements and participant-defined safeguards.

  4. Set evidence and stop thresholds by workstream

    Choose measures before deciding to demonstrate: likely use, accessibility, reliability, travel time, service overlap, safety, privacy, cost and community impact. Write down what result would pause or stop each journey.

    Proof to move on: a public measurement plan and decision rule.

  5. Rehearse the full services

    Rehearse the full service stack through simulation, closed-site automated operation and approved public-road or air operation in parallel. Validate booking, boarding, dispatch, handover, disruption and recovery as parts of the automated system.

    Proof to move on: resolved critical failures and verified readiness for the next approved operating stage.

  6. Approve, redesign or stop each stream

    Publish what was learned. Advance every journey that meets its evidence, safety and approval gates; redesign where a practical fix is realistic; and stop a journey without treating the wider program as a failure.

    Proof to move on: an evidence-backed recommendation open to public scrutiny.

Program structure

Coordinate the network, separate the approvals

Passenger and drone services should be planned as distinct but parallel workstreams, each covering multiple priority journeys and building towards dependable regional coverage.

Shared hubs, community direction, local ownership, workforce pathways, data principles and scalable network planning can connect the program. TMR and CASA remain necessary permit and implementation pathways; each workstream’s operators, risk cases, approvals and stop decisions remain separate.

Decision screen

What makes a priority journey demonstration-ready?

Assess every priority journey after community and service-provider research. “Unknown” is a useful answer; it shows what work remains before real-world operation.

Questions for comparing journey scenarios
Question Evidence to look for Warning sign
Is there a defined gap? People describe the same journey barrier, and the current options map confirms it. The scenario rests mainly on regional statistics or technology enthusiasm.
Would this add value? A practical service pattern improves access, time, reliability or resilience without displacing a better option. A conventional service could deliver the same benefit more simply.
Can it start safely? There is an operationally manageable route, defined operating boundary, qualified operator and credible approvals path. The case depends on broad autonomy, uncertain sites or unresolved regulator questions.
Can everyone take part? Access needs shape vehicles, stops, booking, assistance, information and feedback. Accessibility is deferred until after a technology trial.
Can we stop cleanly? Thresholds, pause powers, data ownership and exit arrangements are agreed in advance. Success is defined as continuing regardless of results.
