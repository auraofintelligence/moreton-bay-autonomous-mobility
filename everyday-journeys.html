Start with trips people already need

The value is not the novelty of a driverless vehicle. It is whether somebody can complete a useful trip more easily, reliably and affordably.

See the journeysSee the network

Four practical journey cases

Ferry terminal connection

Problem: parking is tight and the ferry is not the start or end of the trip.

Service: booked rides between terminals, neighbourhoods, accommodation, work and local destinations.

Benefit: fewer car trips to the terminal and an easier journey for locals and visitors.

Health appointment

Problem: fixed services do not always line up with appointments or mobility needs.

Service: an accessible booked trip with clear boarding and support.

Benefit: fewer missed appointments and less reliance on family or a second car.

Local everyday trip

Problem: shops, work, beaches and community places are spread out.

Service: flexible local rides when an ordinary route is not practical.

Benefit: better access across more hours of the day.

Lightweight essential delivery

Problem: a small item can trigger a long or expensive trip.

Service: delivery to an agreed local collection point.

Benefit: faster access without sending a full vehicle for one small item.

Choose priorities by usefulness

Start where the current trip fails often, affects enough people and has a route the technology can actually handle.

  • Does it solve a repeated local problem?
  • Can people with different access needs use it?
  • Does it improve on the realistic alternatives?
  • Can the service recover safely when something changes?
