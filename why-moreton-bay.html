Why Moreton Bay?

Why Moreton Bay can support a region-wide system

People can set the passenger and delivery services they want. Moreton Bay’s varied communities, centres and transport networks create a practical base for coordinated road and drone mobility.

Why here at a glance

People’s direction

People set the direction. The system should serve them, not the other way around.

Proposed regional system

Coordinate road vehicles and drone delivery while giving current transport, delivery and maintenance operators pathways to ownership, work and commercial benefit.

Future service target

Upgrade or replace all suitable local services with a connected network designed for 24/7/365 availability. Scope and approvals are TO BE CONFIRMED.

A journey-first case

Build around multiple journeys people recognise

These are candidate service families within a broader regional system, not limits on ambition. Local evidence must confirm demand, operating needs and the value each service can deliver.

Name the place precisely

The City and the wider bay are not the same project area

A credible proposal must identify its legal and operational geography before drawing routes or suggesting partners.

Verified frame

City of Moreton Bay

This is a defined Queensland local government area. The 2021 Census recorded 476,340 people in the LGA, and Council’s transport strategy applies to its regional transport system.

Proposed program frame: plan a City-wide network linked to public transport, taxis, community transport, local centres and delivery services.

View the official LGA map source

TO BE CONFIRMED

Wider Moreton Bay geography

Journeys across the bay, to islands, or beyond the City boundary may involve different councils, state agencies, landholders, aviation constraints and communities.

No wider route is assumed. Each water crossing, island service or cross-boundary connection needs its own evidence, authorities and community support before joining the program.

Boundary gate

Program boundary, road ownership, stopping places, drone launch and delivery sites, airspace, Traditional Owner engagement pathways, cultural heritage requirements and environmental constraints are all TO BE CONFIRMED.

Implementation context, not ownership

Four practical conditions for making it real

These official sources map the planning and regulatory landscape. They do not create the vision, set people’s direction or approve a local program.

Verified

01

Relevant planning context

Council’s 2042 strategy seeks safe, connected and sustainable transport and considers electric and autonomous vehicles. It is implementation context, not ownership of this proposal.

Verified

02

Existing connections

Bus and train services already form the public network. A regional autonomous mobility program should coordinate with and improve that network during transition.

Verified

03

Queensland precedent

The Queensland Government has used pilots to explore connected and automated transport safety technology and public understanding.

Verified

04

Known approval pathway

CASA treats drone delivery as an advanced operation. A safety case and applicable approvals would be required; Moreton Bay is not a listed approved delivery location.

Evidence before routes

What regional program design must confirm

A working network map grows from local evidence and regional coordination.

Core evidence for the journey and delivery program
Question Evidence needed Current status
Is there a real gap? Journey interviews, existing timetable and access audit, frequency and consequence of the problem. TO BE CONFIRMED
Is autonomy the best tool? Comparison with walking, cycling, conventional shuttle, community transport, service changes and ordinary delivery. Proposed test
Can it operate safely and lawfully? Implementation profiles, route and site surveys, hazards, weather, communications, human fallback, emergency response, permits and regulator advice. Future gate
Who participates and benefits? Transition pathways for taxi, bus, community transport, delivery and maintenance operators, including workforce, ownership and commercial models. TO BE CONFIRMED
Does the place support it? Community review, accessibility testing, cultural authority, landholder agreement, privacy, noise and environmental assessment. TO BE CONFIRMED

A practical program shortlist

  • Names multiple journey priorities and the people they serve.
  • Defines the relevant City and wider-bay operating areas.
  • Maps existing alternatives and useful connections.
  • Identifies approvals for each road and drone workstream.
  • Names the actual permit and legislative decision-makers.
  • Creates operator ownership, workforce and commercial pathways.
  • Sets operating conditions and public review points.
