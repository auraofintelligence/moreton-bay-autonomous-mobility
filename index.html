A people-led bet on the direction of travel

Make the move—and compound Moreton Bay's advantage

Autonomous mobility has crossed from experiment into commercial service. The practical question is when Moreton Bay enters, then how deliberately we build the permissions, infrastructure, skills, ownership and operating knowledge that make every next move stronger.

At a glance

The direction has changed

Paid driverless services now operate across multiple cities and countries. The question is no longer whether the capability will matter, but when and how this region enters.

People choose the bet

Self-sovereign people set the service goal. Institutions provide lawful implementation, permit and legislative pathways; they do not own the vision.

Build a compounding position

Each approval, operating kilometre, trained worker, local hub and reinvested dollar should make the next service easier, safer and more valuable to deliver.

Trajectory, timing, compounding

The argument is not one perfect car. It is the curve.

Driverless systems still crash, stall and make mistakes. Waymo now combines commercial rider-only scale with strong per-distance safety evidence inside defined operating areas. Nevada’s dated August 2026 decision authorised first-year fleet ceilings of up to 5,000 Tesla vehicles and 1,000 Waymo vehicles; these are conditional maxima, not deployed fleet counts.

The strategic choice is whether Moreton Bay waits to buy a finished service from somewhere else, or enters while it can still shape implementation profiles, build local operating capability and keep ownership and profit in the region.

Guide to action

Four decisions that turn the proposal into operations

Each gate produces practical evidence for program design, approvals, delivery and public accountability.

  1. People set the service direction

    TO BE CONFIRMEDPeople, places, existing services and operators

    Publish the regional priorities people choose and how they connect with current transport, delivery and community services.

  2. Design meaningful demonstrations

    ProposedRoutes, vehicles, drone corridors and operating plans

    Advance the road and drone workstreams in parallel where practical, at a scale that demonstrates useful passenger and delivery capability.

  3. Overcomply and secure approvals

    Future gateSafety, access, privacy, implementation profiles and permits

    Design above minimum safeguards, follow each implementation profile and present the case directly to the actual permit and legislative decision-makers.

  4. Operate, publish and compound

    Future gateEvidence for delivery and expansion

    Share results, incidents and community feedback, then turn each operating gain into better approvals, stronger local capability and wider 24/7/365 coverage.

Ground rules

  • Walking, cycling and public transport remain part of the connected system.
  • Accessible non-digital booking must be considered.
  • Human support and a safe fallback must be clear.
  • Privacy, noise and environmental impacts must be measured.
  • Existing operators need pathways to ownership, work and commercial benefit.
  • Institutions handle lawful implementation and approvals; people own the direction.
  • No route, operator or partner is assumed.
