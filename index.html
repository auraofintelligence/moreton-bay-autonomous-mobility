The Bay Islands are a small version of the world to come

Ferries, limited parking, locals, visitors, deliveries and everyday services collide here every day. That makes the islands a real version of the transport problems the world is about to face.

If driverless systems become safer and more reliable than people, when does refusing to use them become the risk? This site is for people who want to understand the trade-offs, learn the system and have a say before choices are imposed on us.

At a glance

The local problem

Ferries connect the islands, but ferry-terminal parking is tight and getting people to and from the terminal can be hard for locals and visitors.

The global shift

Paid driverless services now operate across multiple cities and countries. The technology is changing quickly, but each place still needs its own answer.

The choice

Learn how the systems work, what they cost, what they improve and what they compromise, then decide what you want your community to do.

Trajectory, timing, compounding

The argument is not one perfect car. It is the curve.

Driverless systems still crash, stall and make mistakes. Waymo now combines commercial rider-only scale with strong per-distance safety evidence inside defined operating areas. Nevada’s dated August 2026 decision authorised first-year fleet ceilings of up to 5,000 Tesla vehicles and 1,000 Waymo vehicles; these are conditional maxima, not deployed fleet counts.

The strategic choice is whether Moreton Bay waits to buy a finished service from somewhere else, or enters while it can still shape implementation profiles, build local operating capability and keep ownership and profit in the region.

Guide to action

Four choices that can turn the proposal into a public program

Each choice can produce practical evidence for program design, approvals, delivery and public accountability.

  1. People set the service direction

    TO BE CONFIRMEDPeople, places, existing services and operators

    Publish the regional priorities people choose and how they connect with current transport, delivery and community services.

  2. Design meaningful demonstrations

    ProposedRoutes, vehicles, drone corridors and operating plans

    Advance the road and drone workstreams in parallel where practical, at a scale that demonstrates useful passenger and delivery capability.

  3. Build the case for approvals

    Later decisionSafety, access, privacy, implementation profiles and permits

    Build a case that meets the relevant implementation profiles and can go beyond them where people choose to do so. Take it directly to the people who can issue the permit or change the law.

  4. Operate, publish and compound

    Later decisionEvidence for delivery and expansion

    Share results, incidents and community feedback, then turn each operating gain into better approvals, stronger local capability and wider 24/7/365 coverage.
