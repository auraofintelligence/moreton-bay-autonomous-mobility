The proposed network

Two services. One human-led operating system.

Passenger shuttles would move people on approved road routes while delivery drones move lightweight items on separately approved flight paths. These are distinct, parallel workstreams within a proposed regional system designed towards dependable 24/7/365 service wherever the model proves suitable. Both require practical operations, evidence, permissions and people accountable for every trip.

Proposal

This is an independent, community-facing proposal, not an operating service or announced government program. Local people are the source of its purpose and direction; institutions provide necessary implementation, permit and legislative pathways.

On the road

Accessible electric passenger shuttles planned as a regional service, with human oversight and service support matched to each approved operating model and a 24/7/365 operating target.

In the air

Lightweight deliveries planned as a dependable regional service on approved flight paths under a certified drone operator’s control.

Behind both

Local hubs, accessible booking, charging, monitoring, incident response, local ownership pathways and network planning that can scale with proven demand.

Parallel workstreams, coordinated program

A shared purpose does not mean shared approval

The shuttle and drone workstreams could advance in parallel and share booking support, local hubs, network planning and community governance. Existing transport and delivery operators, workers and service organisations would be invited into ownership, partnership and operating roles so capability, value and profit can remain local. No participant is confirmed. Each workstream’s vehicles, rules, risk cases, permits and emergency procedures would remain separate.

Passenger shuttle

From request to safe arrival

This is a proposed service flow, not a claim about a chosen vehicle or route. In a full regional network, every trip would sit inside coordinated route planning, shift coverage, accessible booking, fleet readiness and service recovery. Each step would need to be tested with passengers, road managers, accessibility advisers and the responsible operator.

Request

A passenger books online or phones a local service desk. They can ask for boarding or accessibility support.

Confirm

The system confirms a time, an agreed hub or stop, the fare if any, and what assistance will be available.

Travel

Under Queensland’s current public-road trial pathway, an expert driver supervises and can take control. Later rider-only service would require its own lawful pathway, operating approval, human support and recovery arrangements.

Complete

The passenger reaches the agreed stop. Staff record service and safety data without collecting more personal data than needed.

The vehicle does not decide whether a person is safe to travel

Human staff would handle assistance requests, unusual conditions, welfare concerns and service recovery. Emergency services remain the emergency response pathway.

Delivery drone

From approved sender to controlled handover

The delivery workstream would establish repeatable, operationally meaningful services between known sites, with dispatch coverage, aircraft readiness and receiving capacity planned as a regional network. Health-related goods would need additional handling, privacy, temperature and chain-of-custody controls set by qualified service partners.

Validate

An authorised sender confirms the item, recipient, packaging and whether it is permitted for the service.

Plan

Trained staff check weather, aircraft status, airspace, route limits, landing or lowering point and current approvals.

Supervise

A licensed remote pilot operates under the certified operator’s procedures, with live monitoring and contingency actions.

Hand over

The item reaches a controlled receiving point. Staff confirm delivery and handle any failed or interrupted flight safely.

No casual drop-offs

A delivery would not be left wherever a map pin lands. Every real-world demonstration would need defined delivery points, safe separation from people and roads, permission to use each site, and procedures for rejected, delayed or undeliverable items.

Operating backbone

What a full regional operating network needs

The 24/7/365 target depends on ordinary, dependable support behind the technology from the first demonstrations through to scalable operations.

01

Hubs and stops

Proposed hubs would provide safe boarding, drone dispatch or receipt, secure storage, accessible paths, lighting and staff access. No site is selected; landowner permission and local planning come first.

02

Booking and phone access

People could book digitally or by phone. A person should not need a smartphone, bank card or technical confidence to ask a question or use an eligible service.

03

Charging and readiness

Managed charging, fleet rotation, maintenance capacity, spare vehicles and fault recovery would support continuous service. Staff would check batteries, sensors, software status, weather limits and maintenance records before release.

04

Human operations

Existing operators and workers would be offered practical pathways to help own, build and run the network. Named operators would roster trained drivers, remote pilots, dispatchers, technicians and support staff; watch live conditions; respond to passengers; and decide whether a trip should proceed.

05

Emergency response

Each system would have its own emergency plan, contact tree, safe-stop procedure, incident preservation process and clear escalation to emergency services and regulators.

06

Evidence and review

Safety events, accessibility, reliability, community feedback and service use would be reviewed at agreed gates. Each workstream pauses when its stop condition is met, without automatically halting sound work in the other stream.

Permission before operation

Two regulatory gates

People determine whether the proposed system is worth pursuing; these institutions do not supply its civic authority or purpose. They do supply necessary legal permissions, implementation pathways and operating conditions. Requirements must be confirmed against each final vehicle, route and operating model. These are starting points, not project approvals.

Road gate

Queensland automated-vehicle trial permit

Queensland says vehicles currently available on its roads are Level 0 to Level 2. For the proposed Level 3 or above trial on a public road, the current Queensland pathway requires a permit and a nominated expert driver.

Read Queensland’s automated-vehicle guidance and trial permit application requirements.

Air gate

CASA operator and flight approvals

CASA treats drone delivery as an advanced operation. A proposed beyond-visual-line-of-sight flight requires a remotely piloted aircraft operator’s certificate, appropriately qualified remote-pilot arrangements and a BVLOS flight approval with operating conditions.

Read CASA’s BVLOS requirements and advanced-operations pathway.

What could this help someone do?

The technology matters only if it supports real, chosen journeys and helps build a dependable regional system. Suitable existing services should be upgraded or replaced with their operators and workers included wherever practical, not pushed aside while value leaves the region.
