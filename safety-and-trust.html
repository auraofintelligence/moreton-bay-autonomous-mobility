Safer, not flawless

Use the evidence. Overcomply for the place.

Waymo’s published rider-only data now show materially lower crash and injury rates per distance than matched human-driving benchmarks inside its operating areas. That changes the starting point. Moreton Bay still needs exact implementation profiles, separate road and air approvals, accessible design and emergency readiness.

The threshold has moved

For a leading rider-only service, safer per kilometre than matched human driving is now an observed result, not a future promise.

Safer does not mean perfect

Driverless vehicles can still crash, stall and make mistakes. Honest reporting, recovery and continuous improvement remain essential.

Overcompliance compounds

Every stronger implementation profile can improve safety, approvals, trust and the speed of responsible expansion.

What the strongest public comparison shows

Safer per distance is now evidence, not speculation

Waymo reports 220.6 million rider-only miles, about 355 million kilometres, through 31 March 2026 across five United States city operating areas. Against human-driving benchmarks adjusted for the same places and road types, its vehicles recorded 94% fewer serious-injury-or-worse crashes, 82% fewer any-injury crashes and 82% fewer airbag-deployment crashes.

What this proves, and what it does not

This is strong evidence that a leading deployed driverless system can outperform human driving per distance inside defined surface-street operating areas. It is not proof that every autonomous vehicle, company, road or weather condition is equally safe. Tesla's published 7× and 5× comparisons concern driver-supervised FSD and should not be presented as equivalent robotaxi evidence.

Separate responsibilities

One concept, two safety cases

The regulator, evidence and operating conditions are different for the road and air parts. The two workstreams can progress in parallel, but each corridor and use case needs its exact legal pathway confirmed with the responsible agencies.

Road pathway to be confirmed

Autonomous road shuttle

Queensland says a permit is required to trial a highly automated vehicle on Queensland roads. Its current public guidance also requires a nominated expert driver. That is a trial pathway, not permission for an unattended public service.

  • Engage the Queensland Department of Transport and Main Roads early.
  • Prepare the trial application, safety management plan, insurance and data-sharing commitments.
  • Check vehicle registration, road-owner, traffic, stop, charging and site requirements against the relevant authority.
  • Demonstrate safe fallback, supervision, incident response and recovery for the intended roads.

Queensland automated-vehicle trial guidance

Air pathway to be confirmed

Delivery drone

CASA requirements depend on the aircraft, operator, route, airspace and operating method. A commercial delivery concept is not made lawful by calling it a trial, and a remotely piloted aircraft operator’s certificate (ReOC) does not by itself approve every operation.

  • Check whether the proposed operation needs a certified operator and licensed remote pilots.
  • Identify every permission needed for the real operation, including any complex operation outside standard conditions.
  • Confirm launch, landing, overflight, land-owner, planning, noise and marine-park constraints separately.
  • Demonstrate containment, lost-link response, weather limits, incident reporting and safe recovery.

CASA Part 101 guidance

A permit only covers the operation it approves

Any approval could carry tight conditions. The proposed service would still need land access, environmental permissions, procurement authority, community safeguards and a responsible operating entity.

Proposed baseline

Eight non-negotiables

These are topics people can include in each implementation profile and operating workstream. They do not claim that a provider, corridor or authority already meets them.

1

Accessible from the start

Co-design with people with disability, older people and carers. Provide step-free boarding, usable space, clear audio and visual information, assistance-animal access and a non-digital booking option. Test against the applicable transport and discrimination law.

2

Real emergency response

Define who answers, who can stop the system, who attends the scene and how passengers or parcels are recovered. Practise loss of power, communications, positioning and weather scenarios with emergency services and local responders.

3

Privacy by design

A privacy approach could collect the minimum personal data, separate safety telemetry from identity, set short retention periods and publish who can access what. It can offer an offline choice and avoid making facial recognition the general way to access a service.

4

Cyber security by design

Threat-model the vehicle, aircraft, fleet system, communications, updates and suppliers. Require secure defaults, strong access controls, signed updates, logging, vulnerability reporting, patch timeframes and a tested cyber incident plan.

5

Noise measured locally

Record a baseline, then measure frequency, duration and character at homes, schools, health facilities and wildlife areas. Publish operating-hour limits and a community complaint pathway alongside any federal drone-noise approval.

6

Country, habitat and land respected

Site selection depends on cultural authority, site control, planning law and marine-park zoning. Sensitive habitat and shorebird disturbance are real factors for people to weigh. A marine-park permission, where needed, is assessed on its merits and cannot be assumed.

7

Complaints lead to change

Accept phone, online and in-person reports; give a receipt; triage safety issues immediately; publish response times and an escalation route; and report de-identified themes and resulting changes.

8

Independent evaluation

An evaluation plan can name its measures and pause points, give evaluators access to relevant operational data, disclose conflicts, include community evidence and publish findings whether the result supports stopping, adapting or scaling.

People need a clear door

What happens when something goes wrong?

Regulator reporting and a local service complaint are not interchangeable. A plain-language operating plan can show both before the first public journey.

Make safe

Stop or contain the operation, protect people and call emergency services where needed.

Notify

Where an authority sets a timeframe, notify the responsible operator, project safety lead, affected people and regulator within it.

Investigate

Preserve logs, use an independent reviewer for serious events and protect personal information.

Learn in public

Publish a de-identified finding, corrective action, completion evidence and the decision to stop, adapt or resume.

No blank cheques

Open questions for evidence to answer

These are not minor details. Until a named owner produces credible evidence, the honest status is TO BE CONFIRMED.

Independent scorecard

What people may want measured and published

A positive press release is not evaluation. A credible evaluation can agree measures before operation starts, compare them with a relevant baseline and report limitations.

Safety and reliability

Incidents, near misses, interventions, failed or cancelled trips, communications loss, weather stops and recovery time.

Access and usefulness

Who could and could not use it, completed journeys, wait time, support needs, missed connections and non-digital access.

Community impact

Noise exposure, complaints, response time, privacy events, traffic interaction, habitat disturbance and local experience.

Cost and capacity

Whole-of-service cost, public contribution, staffing, maintenance, infrastructure, utilisation and cost per completed task.

Environmental effect

Energy source and use, embodied impacts, vehicle kilometres, avoided journeys and impacts shifted elsewhere.

Decision quality

Whether agreed pause points were honoured, dissent recorded, conflicts disclosed, evidence released and corrective actions closed.
