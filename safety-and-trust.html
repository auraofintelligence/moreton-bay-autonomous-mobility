Safer does not mean flawless

Leading driverless systems now report lower crash and injury rates per distance than human benchmarks inside defined operating areas. They still stall, make mistakes and need a clear response when something goes wrong.

What still matters locally

Access

Booking, boarding, information and support need to work for people with disability, mobility devices, limited digital access or low confidence with the system.

Emergency response

Passengers and responders need a plain way to stop, contact help and recover a failed trip.

Privacy and cyber security

Collect only useful data, protect it, limit access and plan for a breach.

Noise and habitat

Aircraft routes and hours need to fit homes, wildlife, weather and Country along the route.

Public incident reporting

Problems, interruptions and fixes need to be visible enough for people to judge the service honestly.

When something goes wrong

  1. Move the vehicle or aircraft into its safest available state.
  2. Tell the passenger, receiver and support team what is happening.
  3. Send human help or another service when needed.
  4. Record the event, fix the cause and publish what happened, what changed and what remains unknown.

Road vehicles and drones follow separate processes

Queensland road permissions and Australian aviation permissions are separate. The exact route, vehicle, aircraft and way it operates determine what applies.
