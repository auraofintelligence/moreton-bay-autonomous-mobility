## What this project is

Moreton Bay Autonomous Mobility is a highly visual public guide and action plan for upgrading passenger and delivery services across Moreton Bay, with existing operators included in ownership, work and profit.

The service target is useful **24/7/365 coverage** wherever needs, operating conditions and approvals support it. The plan is deliberately larger than one token route or technology demonstration. It develops multiple corridors and use cases as one coordinated regional portfolio.

The proposed transition includes today’s taxi, bus, community-transport, delivery, dispatch, maintenance and support operators. The aim is to create pathways for local ownership, work, capability and profit rather than importing a complete closed service that rents the region’s mobility back to it.

## The central case

The main argument is not that any autonomous vehicle is flawless. It is the direction and rate of change:

1. Paid driverless services now operate across multiple cities and countries.
2. Leading deployed systems have accumulated commercial scale and strong per-distance safety evidence inside defined operating areas.
3. Regulators are approving fleet-scale operating envelopes, including Nevada’s August 2026 first-year ceilings of 5,000 Tesla vehicles and 1,000 Waymo vehicles.
4. Vehicle capability, regulation, infrastructure and public familiarity are improving together.
5. The practical decision is when Moreton Bay makes the bet and which local assets make that bet compound.

The project therefore concentrates on assets the region can keep improving:

- implementation profiles that turn rules into exact operating requirements;
- approved corridors, hubs, stops, charging and delivery points;
- local operations, maintenance, accessibility and emergency capability;
- reusable safety and service evidence;
- trained workers and participating local operators;
- local ownership, revenue and reinvestment; and
- multi-vendor procurement that avoids permanent lock-in.

## What it does not claim

The site does not claim a confirmed route, operator, partner, vehicle supplier, budget, permit, land right, cultural authority or government program.

Named agencies and officeholders are shown because they handle particular legal decisions, not because they own or endorse the vision.

Self-sovereign people decide what they support, use, fund and build. Councils, regulators, departments, ministers and parliaments handle different legal decisions about roads, land, procurement, permits, operating conditions and legislative change. No single office controls the whole proposal.

## Research and evidence discipline

The current research cut-off is **26 August 2026**.

Current-status claims were checked live against dated primary sources. Remembered claims and undated summaries are not treated as evidence. An official publisher establishes provenance, not freshness: current law, dated decisions and orders, live registers, regulatory filings and current service-status pages take priority over older explanatory pages.

The site separates current evidence, this proposal and facts that still need checking. Detailed sources sit in one dated library instead of being repeated across every page.

Company statements are labelled as company-reported evidence. Safety comparisons preserve the vehicle mode, operating area, distance, outcome and human benchmark rather than flattening unlike systems into one league table.

## Two coordinated operating workstreams

### Autonomous passenger services

The road workstream covers accessible vehicles, booking, stops and hubs, passenger support, remote assistance, maintenance, incident response and safe recovery. Queensland’s current public-road trial pathway is not presented as permission for a future rider-only commercial network; each later operating model requires its own lawful pathway.

### Lightweight delivery by air

The drone workstream covers qualified operators and pilots, aircraft, beyond-visual-line-of-sight approval, operational volumes, emergency plans, ground sites, noise, environment, land access and marine-park requirements where relevant.

The two workstreams can advance in parallel, but a road approval does not authorise a drone operation and a CASA approval does not grant local land, noise, planning or marine-park permission.

## Implementation approach

The action plan uses exact decision profiles rather than vague requests to “support innovation”. Each profile identifies:

- the service and operating domain being requested;
- the current law, standard, implementation guide or permit pathway;
- the responsible legal entity and actual decision-making office;
- safety, access, privacy, environmental and recovery controls;
- insurance, reporting, data and evidence requirements;
- the unresolved barrier and who can change it; and
- the asset or knowledge that should be retained for the next corridor.

The goal is to overcomply, ask for the full decision required and make each approval improve the next application.

## Preview on a Windows laptop

Open PowerShell in this repository and run:

```powershell
py -m http.server 8000
```

Then open [http://localhost:8000](http://localhost:8000) in a browser. Press `Ctrl+C` in PowerShell when finished.

No package installation or build step is required to view the static site.

## Validate before publishing

On a fresh Windows PowerShell installation, use the executable form of npm:

```powershell
npm.cmd run check
node --check assets/js/site.js
git diff --check
```

The site check verifies the expected pages and unique heroes, unique titles and descriptions, local files and fragments, image dimensions and alternative text, proposal disclosures, canonical public URLs, the visitor and XML sitemaps, footer access to the licence and repository, and accidental local paths.

Manual browser checks should cover desktop and phone layouts, the collapsed menu, page-level horizontal overflow, scrollable evidence tables, keyboard focus, reduced motion and every hero crop.

## GitHub Pages publication

GitHub Pages is configured at [the public website](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/moreton-bay-autonomous-mobility/).

The `main` branch is the source record. The `gh-pages` branch is the public Pages source. Publish the same verified commit to both:

```powershell
git push origin main
git push origin main:gh-pages
```

After every push, confirm that local `HEAD`, `origin/main` and `origin/gh-pages` identify the intended commit, the GitHub Pages build reports `completed/success`, and anonymous requests return HTTP 200 for every page and key image.

Do not describe a change as published merely because it exists locally or on `main`.

## Images and provenance

The logo, favicons and full-width hero images are original raster concept artwork made with the built-in OpenAI image-generation tool. The vehicle scenes use recognisable current robotaxi design forms, including sensor-equipped Waymo-style vehicles, Tesla Cybercab and relevant Chinese robotaxi forms, without logos, wordmarks or claims of supplier participation.

They remain concept images, not photographs of an operating Moreton Bay service. Every page carrying a hero repeats that disclosure. The generation and replacement record is in [`assets/images/IMAGE-PROMPTS.md`](assets/images/IMAGE-PROMPTS.md).

No SVG logo or favicon is used.
