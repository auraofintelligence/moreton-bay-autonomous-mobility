The trajectory is the argument

The decision is not whether one vehicle is perfect. It is whether autonomous services are improving quickly enough that waiting becomes the bigger risk.

Three facts changed the calculation

Research checked 26 August 2026.

20m+

Waymo lifetime rider-only trips reported.

94% fewer

Serious or fatal injury crashes in Waymo's published 220.6 million rider-only mile comparison.

5,000 + 1,000

First-year fleet ceilings Nevada approved for Tesla and Waymo. These are limits, not deployed fleet counts.

This is already an international field

American and Chinese companies are operating, expanding or preparing paid driverless services. Their claims are not identical, so the source library keeps the detail separate.

Waymo

More than 20 million rider-only trips and a large published safety comparison across its US operating areas.

Tesla

Paid Robotaxi service is expanding. Nevada authorised a first-year ceiling of 5,000 fully autonomous vehicles once its permit conditions are met.

Baidu Apollo Go

Fully driverless commercial rides in Dubai and a reported footprint of 28 cities.

Pony.ai

A reported fleet of 1,975 robotaxis at 30 June 2026, with fare-charging services in China and public service in Singapore.

WeRide

Paid fully driverless Uber rides in Dubai and more than 200 robotaxis reported across the Middle East.

Zoox

Public rides in its purpose-built driverless vehicle in Las Vegas.

What the curve means for Moreton Bay

Build local knowledge, infrastructure and operator experience before buying a fleet.

  • Learn several vehicle and aircraft systems.
  • Turn local routes into reusable operating knowledge.
  • Give existing operators a path into ownership, work and profit.
  • Use each real service result to improve the next decision.

The timing question

Enter too early and the region carries avoidable cost. Enter too late and it rents back the technology, data and operating knowledge from somebody else.
