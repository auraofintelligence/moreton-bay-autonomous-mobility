Turn timing into regional advantage

Mobilise, operate and compound

This is a self-sovereign people’s implementation plan: enter the global shift while Moreton Bay can still shape it, build towards useful 24/7/365 coverage, and make every approval, operating kilometre, trained worker and local asset improve the next move.

Decision pathway

Eight proposed decision points

The named owners below are possible roles, not claims that any organisation has agreed to participate. Workstreams can progress in parallel when the people involved decide they are ready and the relevant authority is in place.

  1. Proposed decision 1

    Set a people’s service charter

    Owner: proposed community convenor and future lawful operating entity Output: people’s service charter and operating-accountability model

    Describe the goal of a region-wide, accessible network available 24/7/365 where service needs, operating conditions and approvals allow. Let people choose public benefits, decision rights, conflict handling, cultural and community review points, funding boundaries and reasons to pause or change the work.

    Evidence for the next decision: a published draft charter, documented community review, possible accountable roles, declared interests and a clear statement that no mandate, provider or partner is being claimed.

  2. Proposed decision 2

    Design the regional service portfolio

    Owner: proposed community and service-design group Output: network needs, corridors and use-case map

    Map health, work, education, community, island-mainland and goods-delivery needs across Moreton Bay. Identify where autonomous services could extend, upgrade or replace suitable service models, and how they could connect with public transport and existing local operations.

    Evidence for the next decision: a regional coverage baseline, multiple priority use cases and corridor options, participation gaps, dissent, reasons to pause, non-digital access options and measurable service outcomes.

  3. Proposed decision 3

    Create a local transition and ownership compact

    Owner: proposed operator, workforce and community working group Output: transition, ownership and local-benefit options

    Invite existing taxi, bus, community-transport, delivery and maintenance operators to help shape pathways as future owners, partners, workforce, maintainers and local profit participants. Address training, job design, fleet transition, procurement access, revenue sharing and protections for people affected by service change.

    Evidence for the next decision: a published options paper, workforce and skills plan, local ownership and participation models, transition protections and every unconfirmed commitment labelled TO BE CONFIRMED.

  4. Proposed decision 4

    Build implementation profiles and evidence packs

    Owner: proposed road, air, place and assurance leads Output: corridor profiles and over-compliance packs

    Build an implementation profile for each priority corridor and use case. The proposed approach is to overcomply by assembling evidence on safety, access, privacy, cyber security, insurance, workforce, data rights, land, Country, environment, noise, procurement, local operating capacity and safe exit before it is requested.

    Evidence for the next decision: complete profile templates, separate road and air safety evidence, a public approval matrix, independent pre-assessment and every evidence gap visibly labelled.

  5. Proposed decision 5

    Map power, decisions and blockers

    Owner: proposed policy, legal and public-accountability team Output: living power and decision map

    Identify who can decide each permit, policy, budget, procurement, road, land, airspace and legislative question. Distinguish elected decision-makers, departments, regulators, road owners, land managers and future operating entities so advocacy reaches the actual source of power.

    Evidence for the next decision: verified offices and roles, current legal instruments, decision points, application paths, review rights, public-accountability routes and named blockers, without assuming that any person or agency supports the proposal.

  6. Proposed decision 6

    Run the permit and legislative campaign

    Owner: future authorised applicants and proposed public advocacy coalition Output: applications, submissions and reform program

    Pursue existing permit pathways wherever they can authorise the intended operation. Where current law blocks an otherwise justified service model, publish the case for an amendment or new law and target the responsible ministers, agencies and parliamentary processes with evidence-based lobbying. Declare lobbying, funding and conflicts in public.

    Evidence for the next decision: lodged applications and submissions, receipt records, written regulator feedback, a public legislative-change brief where needed, advocacy disclosures and an issues register showing what remains unresolved.

  7. Proposed decision 7

    Run meaningful regional demonstrations

    Owner: proposed operators under safety oversight Output: verified readiness and demonstration reports

    Run road and air commissioning as separate, parallel workstreams. Progress each through simulation, closed-site checks, route proving, staffed exercises and approved public operation, then demonstrate across enough corridors, service hours, demand patterns and weather conditions to produce meaningful evidence.

    Evidence for the next decision: approvals in force, completed test matrices, closed corrective actions, trained local staff, emergency exercise sign-off, published safeguards and independent recommendations for each workstream.

  8. Proposed decision 8

    Roll out the regional network

    Owner: future lawful network entity and independent oversight Output: public evaluation and rollout decisions

    Use demonstration evidence to open and extend approved corridors and service windows towards the 24/7/365 network target. Publish service, safety, access, workforce, local-benefit, incident, complaint and cost results. Make stop, adapt or scale decisions for each corridor and workstream as well as the program as a whole.

    Evidence for the next decision: a complete de-identified dataset, independent evaluation, approvals and funding for each rollout stage, agreed transition terms and a documented stop, adapt or scale decision.

90-day mobilisation

Mobilise the regional program

Use 90 focused days to establish the charter, regional portfolio, local transition compact, implementation profiles, decision map and funded work program. It is a serious mobilisation, not a promise to bypass approvals.

Days 1-30

Convene and charter

  • Recruit a proposed independent convenor and identify lawful operating-entity options.
  • Publish the proposal status, people’s service charter draft, interests register and open questions.
  • Gather regional service needs through accessible online, phone and in-person routes.
  • Invite existing transport, delivery and maintenance operators and workers into transition planning.
Days 31-60

Build the implementation base

  • Develop multiple road-corridor, drone-area and use-case options through paid co-design.
  • Start road and air implementation profiles and over-compliance evidence packs in parallel.
  • Map land, Country, ecology, noise, communications, workforce and infrastructure constraints.
  • Draft the local transition and ownership compact with participation and profit-sharing options.
Days 61-90

Set the delivery campaign

  • Publish the regional portfolio, implementation profiles, constraints, dissent and unanswered questions.
  • Verify the power and decision map and begin targeted regulator and decision-maker engagement.
  • Set the permit, legislative, procurement, safety, demonstration, rollout and evaluation program and budget.
  • Record proceed, adapt or stop decisions for each workstream, with owners and next milestones.

Improve the trajectory as we enter it

Design every move to make the next move stronger

The bet is not a one-off vehicle purchase. It is a compounding regional position. Each workstream can leave behind reusable capability even when a supplier, route or technology changes.

1

Implementation profiles

Turn each corridor’s safety, access, land, Country, insurance, cyber, noise and emergency evidence into a reusable profile for the next corridor.

2

Operating intelligence

Use real service kilometres, interventions, demand and recovery events to improve scheduling, safeguards, procurement and regulatory submissions.

3

Shared infrastructure

Build hubs, charging, communications, dispatch and maintenance capacity that can support multiple vehicles, operators and service types.

4

Local ownership and skills

Equip current workers and give existing operators practical ways to own, run and profit from the new network instead of importing a closed replacement.

5

Permission and law

Use over-complete evidence, demonstrated performance and public accountability to improve permit conditions and make the case for any necessary legislative change.

6

Demand, revenue and reinvestment

Link useful journeys into a fuller day-and-night network, then reinvest local value into longer hours, wider coverage and more resilient service.

Keep the option value

Use multi-vendor procurement, open data rights and interoperable infrastructure so Moreton Bay can switch, combine or replace suppliers without surrendering the regional capability it has built.

Regulatory reality

TMR and CASA do different jobs

No single letter, council motion, grant or operator certificate would approve the whole concept.

Approval reality check
Stream Starting point Do not assume Proof before operation
Queensland road trial TMR’s Automated Vehicle Trial Permit Framework and the national trial guidelines. That “driverless” public operation is generally available. Queensland’s current guidance says a permit and nominated expert driver are required for an automated vehicle trial. Permit and conditions, expert driver arrangements, compliant vehicle and road approvals, safety plan, insurance and authorised route.
Drone operation CASA Part 101, operator and pilot credentials, plus approvals matched to the intended operation. That a remotely piloted aircraft operator’s certificate (ReOC) approves every delivery route, airspace, flight near people or operation outside standard conditions. Current certificates and licences where required, aircraft registration, operation-specific CASA approvals or permissions, manuals, risk assessment and conditions.
Places and impacts Land-owner authority, local and state planning, environment, marine-park zoning, cultural heritage and federal drone-noise requirements. That regulator approval for the vehicle or aircraft grants permission to use a stop, launch site, landing site or overflight path. Written site rights and every applicable planning, environmental, cultural, noise and works decision.
Legislative change A published legal analysis identifying the exact statutory or regulatory barrier and the government with power to change it. That a permit can override legislation, or that lobbying guarantees an amendment or new law. An enacted and commenced amendment, regulation or new law, followed by any approvals it requires.
Public funding and buying A lawfully authorised budget and the relevant government procurement rules. Application-ready implementation profiles

Overcomply before the first formal meeting

Build each evidence pack against the live application checklist, then add the safeguards and operating proof Moreton Bay chooses above the minimum.

Queensland road profile
  • Legal applicant, incorporation, accountable roles and written local-government support.
  • Route maps, operating dates, roads, stops and every relevant road-owner approval.
  • Vehicle, modification, import and Australian Design Rule information.
  • SAE automation level, operating domain, human-machine interface, object detection and fallback.
  • Safety, incident, traffic and change-management plans, including expert-driver arrangements.
  • Passenger-service classification, accessibility and Disability Standards evidence where applicable.
  • Public engagement, journey data, takeover data, complaints and end-of-operation reporting.
  • Comprehensive insurance of at least AU$20 million for the trial, naming the State of Queensland as a party, plus workers compensation insurance.

Use TMR’s current application checklist and discuss eligibility with LAVR@tmr.qld.gov.au.

CASA advanced-drone profile
  • Legal entity and aviation reference number, ReOC and qualified RePL arrangements.
  • Exact coordinates, aircraft type and weight, timeframe, altitude and intended payloads.
  • Operational manual, emergency-response plan and accountable human roles.
  • KML operational volume, ground and air buffers, weather and communications limits.
  • Beyond-visual-line-of-sight flight approval and any permission for operations near people.
  • AC 101-06 AusSORA evidence for new complex or BVLOS applications.
  • Separate land, marine-park, road-corridor, privacy and Commonwealth noise decisions.

Start with CASA’s advanced-operations pathway. CASA warns that BVLOS assessment can take several months.

Decision point

Stop, adapt or scale

The evidence has to be able to change the plan. Decisions can differ by corridor and workstream. “Scale” means extend the approved network towards the regional target with the authority required for each rollout stage.

Stop

End or pause the operation

Use when a critical safety control fails, an approval is breached, a serious privacy or cyber risk is uncontrolled, site or cultural authority is absent, community harm is disproportionate, or the service does not solve a worthwhile need.

Possible response: make safe, notify, preserve evidence, support affected people, investigate independently and publish what can be safely released.

Adapt

Change and test again

Use when the core need remains credible but a route, timetable, interface, accessibility feature, noise limit, staffing model or technical control needs improvement.

Possible response: document the issue and new control, update approvals where needed, repeat the relevant tests and show that corrective action has closed.

Scale

Move into network rollout

Use when safety targets are met, benefits are real and fairly distributed, complaints are managed, costs are credible, permissions are respected and independent evaluation supports extension.

Possible response: consult affected communities, publish the rollout case, secure the relevant procurement and approvals, apply the transition compact and retain agreed pause points.
