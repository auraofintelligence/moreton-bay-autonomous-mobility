# Moreton Bay island transport research register

Research checked: 26 August 2026.

This file preserves the island-by-island transport research supplied at the start of the project and records what could be checked against current public sources. It is a working register, not proof that every local operator is trading today.

Three evidence states are used:

- Current public source: a live government, public transport, ferry operator or service page was checked.
- Project research lead: the service or organisation was identified in the original project research and still needs direct confirmation.
- Historical only: an older road or planning figure that must not be presented as a current measurement.

## Minjerribah / North Stradbroke Island / Straddie

Current public sources confirm SeaLink and Stradbroke Flyer ferry connections from Cleveland to Dunwich, plus Translink routes 880 and 881 between Dunwich, Point Lookout and Amity. SeaLink's current visitor information identifies fuel in Dunwich, Amity and Point Lookout.

Project research identified Stradbroke Cab Service, two 10-seat vans including one wheelchair-accessible vehicle, StradEasy booked transport, Amity Point Community Club and Straddie Sharks courtesy-service leads, the Straddie Chamber and QYAC as a separate local network.

Supplied local observation estimates about 300 to 400 parked vehicles across the One Mile and Junner Street ferry terminals. A dated formal count and exact current named public road kilometres remain unresolved.

## Coochiemudlo

Current public sources confirm the F50 passenger ferry and separate vehicle barge from Victoria Point.

The original project handoff described Coochie Bus as an active volunteer and on-demand service operated by the Coochiemudlo Island Progress Association, with tens of thousands of passenger trips. Present operation and the original patronage source need direct confirmation. The handoff also identified the Coochiemudlo Island Business Association, whose current status needs confirmation.

No current retail fuel outlet has been verified.

Historical only: about 11.8 km of streets, 5.3 km sealed and 6.5 km unsealed.

## Russell / Canaipa

Current public sources confirm Translink passenger ferries and SeaLink vehicle ferries from Redland Bay. A March 2026 ministerial response confirms that earlier Russell and Macleay bus trials had ceased.

Project research identified Russell Island Taxi, Island Ride, a proposed or developing Island Bus, Russell Island Bowls Club courtesy transport, Russell Island RSL courtesy transport, the Russell Island Progress Association and the Southern Moreton Bay Islands Chamber. Current operation needs direct confirmation. Project research also identified a service station on High Street, but a fresh primary source has not been located.

Historical only: a planning footprint of about 97.9 km.

## Macleay and Perulpa

Current public sources confirm the shared Redland Bay passenger and vehicle ferry network. A March 2026 ministerial response states that earlier island bus trials had ceased and Macleay had no on-island public bus service. Perulpa is road-connected to Macleay and can share an operating network while retaining its own geographic identity.

Project research identified Macleay Island Taxi and transport leads connected with Club Macleay, Bay Islands Golf Club, Macleay Island Arts Centre, Pub Paradise and Lions. It also identified the Macleay Island Progress Association, the Southern Moreton Bay Islands Chamber and a Macleay petrol station. Current operation and fuel availability need direct confirmation.

Historical only: a planning footprint of about 56.8 km.

## Lamb / Nguderoo

Current public sources confirm the shared Redland Bay passenger and vehicle ferry network. The Southern Moreton Bay Islands community directory lists Lamb Van for local trips by donation and free recreation-club trips.

Project research also identified the Lamb Island Residents Association and the Southern Moreton Bay Islands Chamber. No current retail fuel outlet has been verified.

Historical only: a road-network figure of about 10.3 km.

## Karragarra

Current public sources confirm the shared Redland Bay passenger and vehicle ferry network. No current island taxi, minibus or courtesy operator was found in current official sources.

Project research identified the Karragarra Island Ratepayers and Residents Association and an earlier autonomous shuttle precedent. No current retail fuel outlet has been verified.

Historical only: about 4.8 km of public roads, 4.1 km sealed and 0.7 km unsealed.

## Mulgumpin / Moreton Island

Current public sources confirm the MiCat vehicle and passenger ferry from the Port of Brisbane and the separate Tangalooma passenger ferry from Pinkenba. Queensland Parks requires suitable high-clearance low-range 4WD vehicles and a vehicle access permit for recreation-area driving. Fuel access is limited.

Project research identified 4WD and tour transfers including Moreton Island Tourist Services. Gheebulum Kunungai National Park and the Moreton Island Recreation Area are jointly managed by the Quandamooka People and Queensland Parks. This does not describe every island road, land parcel or visitor operation.

Do not force one road number onto Mulgumpin. Named conventional roads, named 4WD tracks and legal beach-driving corridors need separate measurement.

## Bribie / Yarun

Bribie is the bridge-connected outer comparison. Current public sources confirm Translink routes 640, 641 and 642 and recreation-area 4WD requirements. Project research identified ordinary taxis, rideshare, fuel and supplies in several locations.

Bribie is useful for comparing regional integration, but it does not represent a ferry-isolated island network.

## Road-data rule

Do not recycle historical planning totals as current road figures.

The required current measure is the total kilometres of named, publicly accessible vehicle roads measured along current road centrelines. Report sealed roads, unsealed roads, named 4WD tracks and legal beach-driving corridors separately. Exclude private, restricted and non-vehicle routes.
