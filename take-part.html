Six useful roles

Where could you help build it?

None of these roles implies endorsement or an existing partnership. Strong participation can advance, improve, redirect or stop a workstream when the evidence requires it.

Community members

Write the people’s service charter

Share recurring journey gaps, places that are hard to reach, the service hours people need, safety concerns, no-go conditions and the public benefits the network should deliver.

  • Describe who, where, when and what currently happens.
  • Include accessibility, cost and non-digital access.
  • Say what success or unacceptable harm would look like.
Service organisations

Define a real service need

Bring de-identified patterns from health, disability, aged care, education, emergency relief, retail or community services.

  • Separate client experience from organisational opinion.
  • Name handover, staffing and duty-of-care requirements.
  • Nominate people who must be included in co-design.
Government and agencies

Map power and decisions

Explain jurisdiction, policy, land, roads, procurement, planning, data, legislation and approval pathways without implying a future decision.

  • Identify the right formal process and decision-maker.
  • Flag conflicts with current plans or legal duties.
  • State which facts need written confirmation.
Operators and suppliers

Build the transition and delivery model

Show how existing operators and workers could participate as owners, partners, operators, maintainers and local profit participants. Explain the operating model, Australian approvals, limitations, safety record, accessibility, data rights and whole-of-life costs.

  • Disclose commercial interests and dependencies.
  • Map claims to regulator or test evidence.
  • Use formal procurement routes when they exist.
Researchers and evaluators

Make learning independent

Propose a baseline, methods, representative participation, data needs and pre-set stop, adapt and scale criteria.

  • Declare funding and conflicts.
  • Include negative and inconclusive results.
  • Plan privacy-safe public data and peer scrutiny.
Funders and partners

Fund the delivery program

Support the service charter, regional mapping, transition compact, implementation profiles, access and safety assurance, permit and legislative work, meaningful demonstrations and independent evaluation.

  • Publish eligibility, conditions and decision rights.
  • Keep operator and evaluator incentives separate.
  • Budget for safe closure as well as launch.

What a useful evidence entry contains

Claim or question · source · date · place · method · limitations · permission to share · reviewer · status. Keep personal information and culturally restricted material out of a public register.

Questions welcome

Questions that should shape the next version

A serious proposal should make uncertainty easy to see and safe to challenge.

Which needs and corridors belong in the regional portfolio?

No priority portfolio has been selected. Evidence should map health, community, education, work, island–mainland and goods-delivery needs across the region, alongside current services and other delivery models.

Who can lawfully carry operating responsibility without owning the vision?

No operating entity has been confirmed. It would need the legal authority, budget control, public accountability, procurement capability and safety responsibility required to contract and operate under the people’s published service charter.

Which Traditional Custodians and land holders must be involved?

That depends on the real route and sites. Cultural authority, land tenure and consent cannot be inferred from a regional map or a general acknowledgement.

How can the road and drone workstreams advance together?

Run them in parallel with separate owners, safety cases and approvals. A delay or redesign in one workstream should not set the ambition or timetable for the other, while shared mapping, access, workforce and public-accountability work continues.

How would a provider be chosen?

No provider is chosen. Any public purchasing would need an authorised and probity-controlled process. A market-led proposal is a specific route with strict eligibility, not a way to bypass competition.

Who pays, and what happens when funding ends?

Funding is not confirmed. A business case should identify public and private contributions, operating subsidy, assets, data ownership, maintenance, decommissioning and a safe exit.

How can someone disagree or raise harm?

A future project should provide phone, online and in-person options, anonymous safety reporting where practicable, response standards, escalation and published de-identified outcomes. Official complaints should still go to the agency responsible for the issue.

The live power map

People own the direction. These offices control particular legal doors.

Lobby the person or office that can actually move the decision, with an implementation profile built for that decision. No mayor, minister, regulator, executive or committee can approve the entire network alone. Officeholders and pathways below were checked on 26 August 2026.

Current decision-makers, legal powers and correct public entry points
Decision Current people and office What they can decide Correct action path
Council policy, local roads, public land and procurement Mayor Cr Peter Flannery, the 12 councillors and CEO Scott Waters, City of Moreton Bay. Councillors collectively make Council policy and resolutions. Council can act on local roads and land, procurement and local laws within State law. The CEO implements Council decisions. Neither the Mayor nor CEO can issue a State AV permit or CASA approval. Contact the Mayor or relevant councillor; use Community Comment, a valid Council petition, or the relevant procurement route.
Queensland AV trial permit and State-road access Hon Brent Mickelberg MP, Minister for Transport and Main Roads; Sally Stannard, TMR Director-General; TMR’s Licensing, Automated Vehicles and Registration team. TMR processes highly automated vehicle trial permits and controls State roads. Separate road-corridor, traffic, passenger-service and local-road decisions may still be needed. The public application pages do not name the individual permit delegate. Send the road implementation profile to LAVR@tmr.qld.gov.au; use the Road Corridor Permit portal where relevant; address policy reform to the Transport Minister.
Queensland legislative change The Transport Minister and Cabinet can authorise a government bill; Queensland’s 93-member Legislative Assembly debates and passes State Acts. No minister, MP, Speaker, committee or Governor acting alone can make the required law. A committee scrutinises and recommends; it does not grant an operating approval. Publish the exact legal barrier and proposed amendment; lobby the responsible minister and MPs; use a Queensland Parliament petition or a bill-specific committee submission when available.
Drone delivery and BVLOS flight approval Pip Spence PSM, CASA CEO and Director of Aviation Safety; CASA National Operations and Standards; Advanced RPA Operations and RPAS application teams. Delegated CASA officers can issue aviation certificates, licences, flight approvals and operating conditions. CASA cannot grant local land, road, marine-park, passenger-service or planning approval. No individual application delegate is publicly named. Start with CASA’s Advanced RPA Operations enquiry and rpas.pac@casa.gov.au, bringing the ReOC, SORA, emergency plan, operational volume and exact service profile.
Commonwealth drone-noise approval Hon Catherine King MP, Commonwealth Infrastructure and Transport Minister; the Department of Infrastructure’s drone-noise approval function. No individual application delegate is publicly named. The department administers the Air Navigation (Aircraft Noise) Regulations 2018. A commercial or complex drone operation may need an automated or full noise approval, with conditions on matters such as hours, aircraft, frequency, reporting and complaints. CASA does not make this noise decision. Complete the live drone-noise self-assessment and application. Take the operation’s aircraft, locations, hours, noise evidence, mitigation, community engagement and complaints plan into any full assessment.
Commonwealth AV policy or law Hon Catherine King MP, Minister for Infrastructure and Transport; the Office of Future Transport Technology; the House of Representatives and Senate. The Minister and department lead policy and can advance a government bill. Both houses must pass a Commonwealth Act in the same form before assent. They cannot issue Queensland road or CASA project permissions by policy announcement. Take the national reform brief to OFTT@infrastructure.gov.au and the Minister’s official contact; make submissions to the relevant parliamentary inquiry or bill committee.
National model law and ministerial coordination Michael Hopkins, NTC CEO and Commissioner; Aaron de Rozario, Regulatory Reform and Emerging Technologies; Australia’s infrastructure and transport ministers. The NTC develops model law and advice. Its model legislation has no force until governments enact it. Transport ministers can coordinate reform direction but do not replace Commonwealth, State or Territory legislation. Send the evidence-based national issue to automatedvehicles@ntc.gov.au and use the Infrastructure and Transport Ministers’ Meeting pathway for cross-government reform.
Moreton Bay Marine Park permission Hon Andrew Powell MP, Queensland Environment Minister; Trish O’Callaghan, department Director-General; Queensland Parks and Wildlife Service and delegated marine-park permit officers. The department’s chief executive or delegate can grant, condition or refuse a marine-park permission consistent with the zoning plan. This does not replace CASA, land, road, planning or noise approvals. Seek pre-lodgement advice from marineparkspermits@detsi.qld.gov.au and use the marine-park application guidance for any relevant route or site.
Overcomply, then ask for the full decision required

Do not ask an office to “support innovation”. Ask for the exact permit, policy, budget, procurement, corridor or legislative decision, attach the matching implementation profile, publish the response and take any unresolved legal barrier to the people who can actually change it.

Use the right door

Practical routes to build support and change decisions

These are existing public pathways, not evidence that City of Moreton Bay, TMR or CASA supports this proposal. Always read the live eligibility rules before acting.

Residents and community

Use current engagement or contact channels

Check Your Say Moreton Bay for a genuinely relevant open consultation. If none applies, use Council’s official online contact form to ask a question, provide feedback or lodge a request.

Keep the request inside Council’s role — for example, ask whether Council would consider a regional needs, corridor and implementation study. Council cannot grant a TMR road-trial permit or a CASA aviation approval.

Ratepayers and residents

Apply for Community Comment

City of Moreton Bay says ratepayers and residents can apply to speak in the Community Comment session of a General meeting. The Chief Executive Officer must receive the completed form seven days before the meeting.

The session is not held at certain first, last and election-period meetings. Check the current meeting schedule, policy and form before preparing to speak. Participation is an opportunity to comment; it is not an approval.

Check Council meetings and Community Comment rules

Community groups

Prepare an eligible petition

Council’s current rules require its adopted template and at least 10 signatories. The principal petitioner must live within Moreton Bay, signatories must be Queensland residents and the request must concern a matter Council has power to act on.

An online-platform petition does not replace Council’s adopted process. Frame a specific Council action — not a request for Council to issue state or federal approvals.

Read Council’s petition criteria and template

Suppliers

Follow open procurement

Read Council’s supplier guidance, register for the marketplace it nominates, monitor current tenders and the contracting plan, and respond only through the stated process. A conversation or demonstration does not create a purchasing commitment.

Read Council’s supplier and procurement guidance

Businesses and organisations

Check Market-led Proposal eligibility

Council describes a Market-led Proposal as an unsolicited private-sector pitch. Its current criteria include strategic alignment, a genuine problem or opportunity, justification for direct negotiation and reasons the solution is unsuitable for normal open procurement. It cannot be a request for a grant.

Read the complete guideline before submitting. Acceptance for assessment would not equal endorsement, funding or a contract.

Read Council’s Market-led Proposal rules

Qualified future operators

Engage regulators on the real operation

For the road workstream, begin with the Queensland trial framework and TMR advice. For the drone workstream, identify the CASA certificate, pilot and operation-specific requirements. Bring corridor implementation profiles, over-compliance evidence packs and an accountable legal entity.

Regulator engagement, public advocacy and legislative lobbying should run in parallel through the correct channels. None replaces community direction, land, procurement, planning, environmental or noise obligations.
